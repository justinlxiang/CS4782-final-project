Motivation, Hypothesis & Targeted Result
  • GPT-2 Medium (354M) gives strong zero-shot LM quality, but full fine-tuning updates every weight and stores a fresh 354M checkpoint per task — costly to train, store, and serve.
  • Adapters (Houlsby et al. 2019) cut trainable params via bottleneck layers but add inference latency; prefix-tuning (Li & Liang 2021) burns context length and is finicky to optimise.
  • LoRA (Hu et al. 2021) freezes W₀ and learns a low-rank update ΔW = BA (rank r ≪ d) on attention Q and V. At inference ΔW folds into W₀ → zero added latency.
  • Targeted result (Hu et al. 2021, Table 4): on E2E NLG, GPT-2 Medium + LoRA at r = 4 on Q,V matches full fine-tuning at ≈ 0.39M trainable params (~1000× reduction): BLEU 68.9 / ROUGE-L 71.3 vs. 68.2 / 71.0 for full FT.
  • Open question. LoRA gives every layer the same rank, yet transformer information specialises with depth. Can we redistribute the same budget for free gains — by hand (Depth-Weighted LoRA) or learned (AdaLoRA-Lite)?
  • Concrete goal. Push the Pareto frontier: beat the uniform r=16 baseline (1.57M trainable params, BLEU 68.21) using a strictly smaller budget — ideally 0.39M (4× fewer params, the uniform r=4 / paper-LoRA size).
  • Bonus question. Single-task LoRA pays a “pick the right adapter” tax at deployment. Can a per-layer router mix N frozen task-specific LoRAs and serve them all from one base? — Mixture-of-LoRA-Experts (MoLE) (Wu et al. 2024).
Hypothesis
If transformer layers specialise with depth, then redistributing the same total rank budget across layers — either with a hand-designed late-layer-heavy schedule (Depth-Weighted LoRA) or an importance-pruned learned allocation (AdaLoRA-Lite) — should match or exceed uniform LoRA at equal (or smaller) trainable-parameter cost, and in particular outperform uniform r=16 with 4× fewer parameters.

Setup & Methodology
Implementation
  • From-scratch PyTorch LoRA — no peft, no loralib.
  • GPT-2 Medium's fused QKV split into independent Q,K,V slices so adapters target Q and V only, matching the paper's rank-deployment ablation.
Data — E2E NLG Challenge
  • 42K train / 4 693 test pairs; structured restaurant meaning representation → natural-language description.
Training
  • AdamW (β=0.9,0.999), lr 2e-4 linear decay, 500 warmup steps, weight decay 0.01.
  • Batch 8, 5 epochs, fp16, ≈ 26K steps per run on a single A100. LoRA: α=32, dropout 0.1, on Q and V.
Generation — official beam decoding
  • Beam = 10, length penalty 0.9, no_repeat_ngram_size = 4, max-new-tokens 64 — paper's E2E config.
Metrics & run inventory
  • Official e2e-metrics: BLEU-4, NIST, METEOR, ROUGE-L, CIDEr (TF-IDF weighted).
  • 5 uniform ranks r ∈ {1, 2, 4, 8, 16} · 3 late-ramp budgets r ∈ {4, 8, 16} · 3 r=4 ablations · 3 AdaLoRA-Lite settings rmax ∈ {6, 8, 16}, all targeting 192 active rank units.

Extension #1 — Depth-Weighted LoRA (Late-Ramp)
  • Hand-designed allocation with the same total rank budget as the paper (192 units across 48 Q/V slices).
  • Redistributed by depth — small / early, larger / late. Same trainable-parameter cost as uniform r = 4.

Extension #2 — AdaLoRA-Lite
  • Don't pick the profile by hand — let the model learn it. Each rank direction has a scalar gate si.
  • EMA = smoothed “typical” importance. Each refresh we measure how much the loss cares about gate i (via |si·∂L/∂si|). That number jumps around across minibatches. The exponential moving average (β = 0.85) keeps a slow-updating memory: mostly the previous score, plus a slice of the new one — like a rolling, denoised track of which gates consistently matter, not a one-off spike.
  • Every 500 steps, rank gates by that EMA and prune the least important along the budget schedule until 192 active units remain.
ΔW = (α / rmax) · B · diag(s) · A
Fig. 3. AdaLoRA-Lite workflow: start with all rmax rank directions live, score them by sensitivity-smoothed gate magnitude during training, and prune to the target budget along a cubic schedule (Fig. 4).
Why this schedule?
  • Warmup at rmax. Hold the full gate budget until tinit so EMA importance estimates and activations stabilise — pruning too early drops ranks before we know what matters.
  • Cubic decay. b(t) leaves capacity high early, then ramps cuts faster toward tfinal — smoother than a hard step or aggressive linear trim, so training does not collapse mid-run.
  • Land on budget before eval. Reach the 192 active-unit target while steps remain so the last epochs actually optimise the pruned network, not an over-capacity skeleton.
Fig. 4. Start at full rmax, smoothly cool to the target rank via a cubic schedule, prune by sensitivity-smoothed gate magnitude. Best run: t_init = 10K, t_final = 21K of 26K total steps.
6
Extension #3 — Mixture-of-LoRA Experts (MoLE)
  • Instead of swapping adapters at inference, a tiny per-layer router picks among N frozen single-task LoRAs at every token.
  • Three frozen experts: E2E, DART, and WebNLG rank-4 Q/V LoRAs (same checkpoints as our paper-aligned runs). Only the router trains: one Linear(d → N) per layer ≈ 0.07M total (24×1 024×3 + bias).
  • Top-1 routing (Switch-style): per token, pick argmax expert k = argmaxi gi(x); contribute only gk · Bk(Akx) so gradients still flow through the gate via the softmax probability. Config mole.routing_mode: top1.
  • Router optimised on the concatenation of three processed train sets (2 epochs, AdamW lr 5e-4).
Δh(x) = scaling · gatek(x) · Bk(Akx),   k = argmaxi gatei(x)
Fig. 5. Router emits g1..3 from x; frozen experts contribute gi·Bi(Aix), summed to Δh. Top-1 keeps only the argmax expert.

Fig. 8. Same active 0.39M budget; both extensions sit above the uniform LoRA frontier. AdaLoRA-Lite rmax=6 reaches BLEU 68.85 — beating uniform r=16 with 4× fewer trainable parameters.
Why the three AdaLoRA-Lite runs separate
  • rmax=6 wins (68.85). 1.5× over-rank, 33% prune; long warmup stabilises EMA before cuts.
  • rmax=8 (68.34). 2× over-rank but tinit=15K leaves too few post-prune steps.
  • rmax=16 (67.67). 4× over-rank, prune starts at step 500, min/slice=0, kills slices before convergence.
  • Takeaway. Late + gentle prune from small over-rank > early + aggressive prune from large over-rank.

Conclusion & Future Work
Conclusions & insight
  • LoRA reproduces. AdaLoRA-Lite rmax=6 lands within 0.05 BLEU of the paper at the same 0.39M active budget — confirming Hu et al. (2021) at <1% of full-FT cost.
  • Allocation > raw rank. Late-ramp r=4 beats uniform r=8 (+0.42 BLEU) with half the params: depth > projection identity > total budget.
  • Learned ≈ hand-designed. AdaLoRA-Lite recovers a late-layer-heavy rank profile automatically (Fig. 11), validating the Depth-Weighted LoRA hypothesis without hand tuning.
  • MoLE (Extension #3). 3 frozen task LoRAs + learned router: one served model for E2E, DART, WebNLG (Fig. 6) — multi-task routing that complements the depth-budget results (Figs. 7–12).
Practical benefits
  • Train cheap. Only ~0.39M adapter params train vs 354M full FT — less memory, smaller checkpoints.
  • No latency tax (merged). ΔW = BA folds into W₀ → same matmul path as dense FT.
  • Swap in prod. One frozen base + tiny per-task adapters; load without redeploying the whole model.
Limitations
  • Q/V only. Adapters touch ~5% of params; MLP / Wfc budget unstudied — depth allocation may not transfer there.
  • One backbone, templated NLG. Validated only on GPT-2 Medium and E2E/DART/WebNLG; scale to 7B+ and harder generation untested.
  • No orthogonality regularizer. Unlike full AdaLoRA, our Lite variant skips the SVD-style P/Q orthogonality penalty (‖P⊤P−I‖F2+‖QQ⊤−I‖F2); singular-value magnitudes can drift, so importance scores are an approximation rather than a calibrated SVD.
Future work
  • Scale to larger backbones. Carry our late-ramp + AdaLoRA-Lite recipe from GPT-2 Medium to GPT-2 Large and Llama-2/3 7B; depth-allocation should hold, but the rank floor and rmax sweet spot likely shift.
  • Reach FFN projections. Our extensions only target Q/V (~5% of params); extend AdaLoRA-Lite gates to Wfc / Wproj in the MLP block and re-derive the late-heavy profile there, where most of the parameter budget actually lives.
  • MoLE + adaptive rank: combine mixture-of-LoRA routing with AdaLoRA-Lite–style rank budgets (per-expert or shared); scale to more experts/tasks.
